**Scalar** value quantifying how much an electric field "pierces" a surface.
Electric flux through closed surfaces is what [[Gauss's Law]] relies on.

If $\vec{E}$ is a field that intersects some surface that is split into infinitesimal areas $d\vec{A}$, then the total electric flux $\phi$ through that surface is simply the line integral $$\phi=\int \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{A}\,.$$
This is a flux integral, so it's getting just the component of the $\vec{E}$ field perpendicular to the surface (in the direction of $d\vec{A}$).

### Net Flux
Vital to [[Gauss's Law]] is the concept of **net flux** through a closed surface. $$\phi_{\text{net}}=\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{A}$$
