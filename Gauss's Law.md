As it turns out, the total charge inside any closed surface (in a vacuum) is $$\epsilon_{0}\phi=q_{\text{enc}}$$
Using the integral expression for $\phi$, $$\epsilon_{0}\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{A}=q_{\text{enc}}$$
This also means if we know $q_{\text{enc}}$, we know something about the flux. If $q$ is positive, then the net flux points out of the surface (overall flowing out), and vice versa.

**Important:** $\mathbf{E}$ is the total electric field through the Gaussian surface, not just from charges within it. However, **charges outside the surface don't contribute to the net flux** because as many lines exit the surface as enter it. Even though $\mathbf{E}$ includes external charges, the flux is only related to the enclosed charge.
